# Marshlight: The Borrowed Bells
Design version 3 — 6 November 2026

## Concept
A top-down 2D marsh rescue using the existing player, duck follower, movement, health, coin collection and five-slot inventory. One level; target first human playthrough 5–10 minutes including exploration and text. This is a target, not a timed human playtest result.

## Core mechanic: borrowed bell weights
Collect three old brass coins in the existing inventory. At each sluice bell, press E to lend three coins: their weight lowers a reed causeway. Cross to the far-side return tray and press E to reclaim those same coins. A latched causeway stays open. Repeat at three increasingly hazardous crossings. Coins cannot be lost on death; open routes and returned payments persist during a run. No timer or irreversible expenditure can strand the player. An insufficient balance gives explicit feedback and changes nothing.

## Story bible
Setting: Rill Marsh, after a storm has silenced the three mechanical bells that guide ducklings home.
Characters:
- Aiken: the existing human player, a junior marsh keeper.
- Duckduckgoose: the existing duck companion, following Aiken and listening for the missing flock.
- Ada: the absent senior keeper, heard through letters and signs; she built the bells to lend coins, never keep them.
Beats:
1. At the keeper's landing, Ada's note asks Aiken and Duckduckgoose to reopen the home route before night.
2. The first bell teaches that a useful coin can be lent and recovered.
3. At the reed archive, Ada's flood note reveals she crossed to shelter the flock; the second bell restores hope.
4. The storm channel asks the player to use the same three coins one final time and avoid patrolling marsh mites.
5. At Lantern Haven, the reunited flock answers Duckduckgoose. Aiken leaves the bells ready for the next traveler.

## Level layout
A new scene cloned from LevelOne; the original scene is preserved. Three connected marsh islands, each with a clearly marked exploration loop and a narrow causeway. Landing -> Reedbank -> Old Archive -> Storm Reach -> Lantern Haven. Solid banks bound the playable area; raised water/reed barriers leave only the three gated crossings. Coin glints guide side loops. Checkpoints before each crossing and at the haven. Five optional notes deepen Ada's story, including the Iteration 1 ledger pocket. Existing enemy sprites provide visible patrol hazards. Hearts use the existing healing pickup.

## Rules and controls
WASD/arrows: existing movement. E: bell/return tray/sign interaction. Space/Enter: advance text or start. Esc: pause/resume. R: restart from checkpoint on defeat (coins and gates retained). Win screen supports a fresh run. F6: development-only checkpoint teleport, no progression grant. F7: development-only pacing overlay.
Win: all three bells latched, all three original trays reclaimed, the archive exchange fully settled, and Aiken reaches Lantern Haven with Duckduckgoose.
Lose: health reaches zero; show defeat screen; restart restores health and positions both characters at the last checkpoint.

## Presentation

Brass mechanisms, lanterns and UI retain readable geometric art under Assets/_Agent/Placeholder/. Muted midnight teal, reed green and warm brass; warm lights signify safety, coral marks hazards. Text-only intro, data-driven dialogue entries, readable objectives, interaction prompt, pause/defeat/ending screens, coin/bell feedback, damage flash and small particles.

## Technical constraints
Legacy Input Manager only (activeInputHandler=0). No external downloads. Serialized references throughout. Existing single RPGGameManager and RPGCameraManager are reused. Player death must reach its existing Die/RespawnAt flow; UI references must be repaired. Inventory gains count/spend methods; existing add/stack behavior is retained. All public API additions and existing-script edits are recorded in PROGRESS.md.

## Iteration 1 design — 3 November 2026
Human baseline: about four minutes for a player who already understood the lend/reclaim mechanic. Keep movement at 1.6, preserve the three bell crossings, and add decisions rather than empty distance or waiting.

### The Old Archive counterweight exchange
A required relay puzzle occupies the existing Storm Reach reed loop after the Old Archive checkpoint. It uses the same three coins, with three cups and three separate return trays:
1. Reed cup borrows two coins at (50, 4.5) and opens the downstream/south arm at x=60. Its two-coin return is in the middle arm at (54.8, -5.8).
2. Spill cup borrows one coin at (50, -4.5) and opens the upstream/north arm at x=53. Its one-coin return is beyond the downstream arm at (61.2, -5.8).
3. Exit cup at (57, -5.8) unlocks only once the reed pair has been reclaimed. Lend that recovered pair again to open the archive latch at x=62.5. Its return at (64.2, -3) restores the pair before the final bell.
The first two cups may be funded in either order. The player can retrieve the spill coin before or after funding the exit cup. The unlocked return trays and gates persist through death. Every deposit is refundable exactly once. The exit cup rejects early payment without taking coins, preventing the two carried coins from being stranded in an unreachable exit tray. Three coins remain conserved across carried coins, uncollected pickups, original bell trays and relay escrow.
The original last bell requires the exchange to be fully settled; the haven still requires all original trays and the complete relay. Gate colliders have no patrol/health component. A future patrol-affecting tool cannot open, fund or bypass them.

### Optional content and story limit
Add exactly two story entries: exchange (a concise instruction plate shown on first relay use) and archive-pocket (an optional dry ledger page just off the existing upper trail at (57.8, 6.8)). The ledger page is its own exploration reward and does not add currency. All nine existing entries remain. Total: eleven. Aiken, Duckduckgoose and Ada remain the only named characters.
The exchange has clear two-coin/one-coin markings and colored mechanical links to the affected gates, using existing placeholder art. No art/audio overhaul, palette manifest, weapon, package download or incoming asset import is part of this iteration.

### Pacing and patrol decision
Target roughly one to two extra minutes: split funding, identifying which remote arm opens, reclaiming/re-lending the pair, and an optional ledger page. No movement slowdown, long empty route, timer, or filler text. Keep patrol behavior; only shift the final patrol track slightly so it does not pass through the new latch. More patrol design is deferred because the coin dependency supplies the new challenge and remains meaningful after Iteration 3's tool.

### Development pacing telemetry
Editor and development builds only. F7 toggles an overlay showing active/reading seconds and deaths for each interval: Landing to Reedbank; Reedbank to Old Archive; Old Archive to Haven; Haven to reunion. Title, pause and defeat waiting are excluded. Deaths and retry time accumulate in the current section. F6 marks a run as teleported and unsuitable for pace estimates. Logs are JSONL under Application.persistentDataPath/Marshlight/Telemetry. A run label distinguishes human, mechanical and regression runs. No telemetry component, overlay, logger, key handler or file writes are compiled into release builds.

### Regression plan
Add permanent Unity Test Framework PlayMode tests without moving the existing scripts into new assemblies: a small reflection fixture bridges the project's predefined Assembly-CSharp. Exercise original loans through death, loans at every checkpoint, all relay loan phases, both funding orders, both retrieval orders, early exit-cup rejection, duplicate rejection, atomic returns and victory replay. Every reachable loan state must still reach victory with exactly three coins. Add telemetry timing/overlay checks. Retain every existing mechanical assertion and extend the walker through the new puzzle and optional page. Capture all checkpoints with F6, then finish one entire title-to-victory walk without F6. Use baseline/current section telemetry plus explicit human decision/reading assumptions to estimate time; do not label that estimate as a measured human playtest.

## Mailbox/signposts and Reedkeeper Sword — 6 November 2026

### Art and UI
Create transparent, crisp pixel sprites in the existing top-down 32-PPU style: KeeperMailbox (32x48), TrailSignpost (32x48), ReedkeeperSword (32x32). Generate the art, then crop/resize with nearest-neighbor sampling and reduce to a shared limited palette. Preserve original outdoor textures and the scene's existing collision layout. Mailbox replaces keeper-note art; four signposts replace the other story-note art. Node IDs, trigger colliders and serialized marker references remain valid. New art stays in Assets/_Agent/Placeholder/. Add one equipped-hand panel inside the existing HUD (no new Canvas/Inventory/manager). It shows ownership/equip status, a persistent inventory icon and Q/J hints. The world blade and swipe arc appear only during an active swing; no sword remains visible while idle.

### Sword and coin economy
The Reedkeeper Sword is Ada's marsh-maintenance blade. Its guard borrows one carried brass coin as a counterweight for each 0.20-second swipe, and returns that same coin afterward. Damage 1, enemy HP 3, cooldown 0.50 seconds, range 1.15 world units, a forward 100-degree arc. No carried coin means no swipe; reclaiming coins enables combat again. No currency is permanently spent or created. Weapon escrow joins carried coins, pickups and existing loan trays in the three-coin conservation invariant.
The sword pickup is placed at (0.55, 2.6) beside the first trail and uses the existing Consumable -> Player -> Inventory pickup flow. Append SWORD to ItemType without changing existing values; emit ItemCollected only after a successful pickup. Pickup auto-equips and introduces one new story entry, sword (twelve total). Q equips/unequips; J or left mouse swipes toward the last movement direction. Equipment survives checkpoint death; replay resets it. WeaponData is a ScriptableObject containing damage, cooldown, range, arc/duration, refundable coin cost, inventory item, sprite, sound and an optional projectile-prefab field (null for this melee sword). Weapon reads that data through serialized scene references.
Pause, dialogue, defeat, unequip and run restart cancel the visual and refund synchronously. If an artificially full inventory prevents a refund, the coin remains explicitly recoverable in weapon escrow, another swipe is rejected, the HUD reports it, and refund retries when capacity becomes available. No silent failures or duplicated refunds. Victory requires weapon escrow to be settled; scene replay resets the entire old economy rather than carrying a refund into a new run.

### Damage and no bypass
Use existing EnemyHitPoints.TakeDamage/IsDead/CurrentHitPoints, preserving its health and death rules. Each enemy is hit at most once per swipe, regardless of collider count or frames. Physics queries reject enemies behind the player, outside range, or behind Blocking colliders. Only EnemyHitPoints targets take damage: bells, gates, reeds, trays and other non-enemy objects are unaffected. All original and relay progress prerequisites remain required even if every patrol is defeated.
Add readable white hit flash, three HP pips and a small particle pulse. Flash clears after 0.12 seconds; HP does not regenerate, patrol movement continues, and zero-HP mites follow their existing death/removal flow until a fresh run. No new stun/knockback system is needed for this damaging sword.
